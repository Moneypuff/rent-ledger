Employer EI is not on their list

Every employer paying EI premiums also owes the employer share, at 1.4× the employee premium. On $30.03 withheld that is $42.04 per pay — $1,093.04 across 2026, owed to the CRA. It may simply be missing from the summary they sent rather than from what they file, but it is the one item worth confirming: nothing else in their figures implies it.

The other gap is a cent. QPIP employer at the published 0.602% gives 2,310.00 × 0.602% = 13.91, where they show 13.90 — $0.26 across the year. Not worth chasing, but this page uses the published rate.

Canada Revenue Agency Revenu Québec Differs from the accountant's statement

Basis. The $2,310.00 biweekly salary and the two income tax amounts are taken as given. Contribution rates come from the published 2026 tables and are cross-checked against their annual maximums; the QHSF and CNESST rates (1.65% and 4.20%) are back-solved from the accountant's per-pay figures, since both are employer-specific.

Rounding. Each period is rounded to the cent independently and annual totals are the sum of those rounded periods, which is how source deductions are actually remitted. This can differ by a cent or two from rate × annual earnings.

Before you remit. Confirm the employer EI line with your accountant. Rates and ceilings change every January. Due dates here roll forward off weekends but do not account for statutory holidays, and the CRA and Revenu Québec assign remitter frequency independently — yours may not both be monthly.
