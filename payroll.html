Parties

Pay schedule

The period count follows the frequency, so the pay calendar can't drift out of step with it. The CRA and Revenu Québec each assign your remitter frequency independently — they are often but not always the same, so they're set separately here. Due dates roll forward off weekends; statutory holidays are not in the table, so check a due date that lands on one.

Salary

A fixed amount each period regardless of hours worked. This is the one figure taken from the source screenshot.

Income tax — you must supply these

Income tax depends on the whole TD1/TP-1015.3-V picture, not on a rate this page could apply, so it is not calculated here. Get the federal amount from the CRA's PDOC and the Québec amount from Revenu Québec's WebRAS, then enter both. Record the salary you ran them at — if the salary later changes, the ledger will tell you the tax figures have gone stale instead of quietly reusing them.

Statutory rates — published 2026 tables

The EI rate is the reduced Québec rate — Québec employees pay less because QPIP covers parental benefits. All of these change every year: re-check them each January.

Employer contributions beyond the payroll deductions

Two employer-only charges on total payroll, both remitted to Revenu Québec alongside the source deductions — CNESST premiums have been collected periodically by Revenu Québec since 2011 rather than billed separately at year end. Neither rate is standard: QHSF depends on your total payroll and sector, and CNESST on your classification unit, so both are employer-specific and neither is included in the totals while it is blank.

Clerical correction

Records what was really deducted when a pay was run on the wrong basis, so the paystubs for those periods show the amounts the employee actually saw. A paystub is a record of a payment that happened — restating it with corrected figures would misrepresent it. Remittances are not affected: the employer owes what should have been withheld regardless. Set the period count to 0 when there is nothing to restate.

Rate self-check

Confirms the rates and ceilings above agree with each other — that the maximum a full-year employee could contribute is what the published tables say it is. This checks internal consistency, not whether the rates themselves are current.
